## Critical assessment

1. **Fail fast on the timing signal before building the hierarchy.** The plan’s noiseless and noisy injection test against a Cramér–Rao bound is too narrow: the next listed nuisance tests include Blazhko and period changes, but they should be in the first realistic end-to-end test, because either can create apparent timing structure ([JOURNAL.md:58](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/JOURNAL.md:58>), [JOURNAL.md:59](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/JOURNAL.md:59>)). Start with a stratified OGLE-only sample: fit seasonal delays, simulate realistic Blazhko and period-change cases on the actual timestamps, and measure bias and false alarms. Proceed only if injected LTTE remains recoverable at the expected amplitude. MACHO should come later: the fetcher stores raw columns and says cleaning and MJD-to-HJD conversion will happen downstream ([fetch_macho.py:5](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/scripts/fetch_macho.py:5>)), but the inspected `io.py` has no `read_macho` function ([io.py:49](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/src/rrlbin/io.py:49>), [io.py:59](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/src/rrlbin/io.py:59>)).

2. **Treat a common harmonic delay as a useful constraint, not a Blazhko veto.** The planned model applies one \(\tau(t)\) to all harmonics ([JOURNAL.md:56](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/JOURNAL.md:56>)). That can test whether the waveform shifts coherently, but the claim that Blazhko phase modulation appears as non-common shifts is not sufficient protection: modulation can also move the fitted phase coherently over a season. Compare common-delay fits against flexible harmonic phase and amplitude variation, and retain ambiguous stars with a modeled nuisance distribution rather than simply rejecting them.

3. **Seasonal O−C points are a diagnostic; they do not solve long-period identifiability.** A quadratic period trend and the short arc of a long-period Kepler orbit can both look smooth and nearly quadratic. For periods approaching or exceeding the roughly 5341-day median baseline reported in the journal ([JOURNAL.md:38](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/JOURNAL.md:38>)), orbital period, eccentricity, and phase may be weakly identified. Fit the original timestamps jointly with pulsation ephemeris terms and orbital motion; report sensitivity as a function of period, and do not count a fitted long-period curve as a measured orbit when the data constrain only curvature.

4. **The false-alarm procedure needs the full search accounted for.** The plan proposes a detection statistic and injection/recovery, but does not specify how it controls the search over roughly 20,000 stars and many orbital periods, eccentricities, and phases ([JOURNAL.md:61](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/JOURNAL.md:61>)). Calibrate the maximum statistic over the entire search using null simulations that preserve each star’s cadence and noise structure. Per-star thresholds alone will not control the survey-wide false discoveries.

5. **Condition detection probability on the data quality and the nuisance process.** The planned \(p_{\rm det}(P_{\rm orb},M_2,e,i\mid\mathrm{star})\) names orbital parameters and the star, but the plan does not say what the star-specific conditioning includes ([JOURNAL.md:61](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/JOURNAL.md:61>)). It needs actual timestamps and seasonal gaps, per-epoch uncertainties and outliers, light-curve shape and amplitude, pulsation period, Blazhko behavior, period changes, crowding/blending, and—when combining surveys—time system, zero point, and band/template effects. The inventory counts epochs and takes first/last times but does not filter measurements before summarizing them ([lc_inventory.py:25](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/scripts/lc_inventory.py:25>), [lc_inventory.py:29](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/scripts/lc_inventory.py:29>)). The OGLE reader returns the three input columns without quality filtering or time conversion ([io.py:49](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/src/rrlbin/io.py:49>)).

6. **Do not interpret a null result as a mass distribution without inclination and selection modeling.** The plan names \(f_{\rm bin}(P,q)\) and eccentricity but does not state priors or how they will be inferred ([JOURNAL.md:62](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/JOURNAL.md:62>)). Specify the primary-mass assumption, isotropic inclinations, orbital phase, eccentricity, and a period and mass-ratio parameterization before interpreting companion masses. A null result should yield completeness-corrected upper limits over explicit \((P,M_2)\) bins or a posterior on binary fraction, including regions with near-zero sensitivity. Otherwise it will mainly constrain the assumed priors.

7. **Time and cross-survey systematics could imitate or erase delays.** OGLE times are documented as HJD−2450000 ([io.py:3](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/src/rrlbin/io.py:3>)); the MACHO fetcher records `dateobs` as MJD and defers conversion ([fetch_macho.py:8](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/scripts/fetch_macho.py:8>), [fetch_macho.py:5](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/scripts/fetch_macho.py:5>)). Establish one documented time standard and verify the conversion empirically before fitting across surveys; a constant offset is not enough if the conversion omits heliocentric/barycentric corrections. Fit survey-specific zero points and band-specific templates rather than treating MACHO B/R and OGLE I as interchangeable. The current inventory combines OGLE-III and IV extrema only ([lc_inventory.py:52](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/scripts/lc_inventory.py:52>), [lc_inventory.py:58](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/scripts/lc_inventory.py:58>)); the journal says OGLE-III files already include OGLE-II epochs for some stars ([JOURNAL.md:32](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/JOURNAL.md:32>), [JOURNAL.md:33](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/JOURNAL.md:33>)). Verify epoch coverage from the light curves themselves and model the gaps; a long endpoint-to-endpoint baseline does not mean continuous orbital phase coverage.

8. **Fetch completion is not validated.** Existing tile files are skipped based only on path existence, and the script prints failed tiles without raising a final error ([fetch_macho.py:77](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/scripts/fetch_macho.py:77>), [fetch_macho.py:85](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/scripts/fetch_macho.py:85>), [fetch_macho.py:96](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/scripts/fetch_macho.py:96>)). A run can therefore appear finished while tiles are missing or a prior partial output is being trusted. Also check TAP row limits: the request sets `MAXREC` to ten million but does not detect a truncated response or verify returned IDs against requested IDs ([fetch_macho.py:36](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/scripts/fetch_macho.py:36>), [fetch_macho.py:49](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/scripts/fetch_macho.py:49>)).

9. **Timing precision and likely reach.** For a sinusoidal approximation with \(P_{\rm puls}\approx0.55\) d, semi-amplitude \(A\approx0.3\)–0.4 mag, 100 well-distributed epochs, and independent \(\sigma=0.05\) mag, the timing error is about 2–3 minutes. A sharp RRab template can improve the photon-noise limit; a rough practical expectation is **tens of seconds to about two minutes per season**, before Blazhko or other correlated systematics. This is an estimate, not a result measured from the project’s data. The journal’s edge-on scale gives 218 s for \(M_2=0.2\,M_\odot\), \(P_{\rm orb}=1000\) d and 453 s at 3000 d; for \(0.07\,M_\odot\), the corresponding amplitudes are 85 s and 177 s ([JOURNAL.md:18](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/JOURNAL.md:18>), [JOURNAL.md:19](</Users/vasilybelokurov/IoA Dropbox/Dr V.A. Belokurov/Code/rrl_lmc_binary/JOURNAL.md:19>)). Thus, high-inclination systems with \(M_2\gtrsim0.2\,M_\odot\) and periods of roughly 1000–5000 days look like the credible initial target. Low-mass companions and periods much longer than the baseline are substantially less secure; lower inclination further reduces the timing amplitude.

The cited OGLE LTTE studies I found search Galactic-bulge stars, including [Hajdu et al. 2015, arXiv:1502.01318](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.01318) and [Hajdu et al. 2021, arXiv:2105.03750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03750). ## Strongest argument against my assessment

The timing fit may do better than the sinusoidal estimate because an RRab’s sharp features and multiple harmonics carry more phase information; several seasons also constrain a coherent orbit jointly. In favorable, unmodulated stars, that could make lower-mass companions detectable. The counterpoint is that correlated phase variation and long-period curvature can erase that gain.
